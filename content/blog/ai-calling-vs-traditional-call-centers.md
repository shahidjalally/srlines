## Use repetition and risk to allocate work

Hours, location and basic routing are good automation candidates when the source data is reliable. Disputes, unusual requests and emotionally difficult calls need staff with judgement and authority. Allow callers to ask for a person early in the conversation.

## Design the transfer

A queue number alone does not define a handoff. Decide how staff see the caller’s purpose, how long the caller waits and what happens if a team is unavailable. Test whether the receiving team can resume the conversation with the context it is authorized to see.

## Compare service quality at the same demand

Take a sample of calls by topic, peak time and outcome. Compare acknowledgement, correct routing, repeat calls and staff follow-up completion. An automated answer that causes another call can create extra work, even when initial answer rates improve.
